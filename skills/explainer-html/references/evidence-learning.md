## 2. Three limits that apply to every finding

1. **Design effects are smaller on self-paced pages.** Noetel et al. 2022 (29 reviews, 1,189 studies, 78,177 participants): good design was more important "in system-paced environments (e.g., lectures) than self-paced ones (e.g., websites)". (V1) https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329
2. **Text stays on the page.** Several effects (segmenting, modality) appear for information that disappears, such as speech. They are typically not found for text. Sweller et al. 2019. (V1)
3. **Mayer's effect sizes come from his own laboratory.** Independent meta-analyses usually give about half the size.

Read the effect sizes below as upper limits for a web page.

## 3. Working memory and cognitive load

| Finding | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Working memory holds about 3 to 5 new items, not 7. | Cowan 2010: "3 to 5 meaningful items". Miller's 7 was "a rough estimate and a rhetorical device". (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X01003922 |
| The limit applies to new ideas that must be held in mind together. It does not apply to the number of visible items. | Sweller 2019. (V1) | https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5.pdf |
| Difficulty of the content changes only if the content or the reader's knowledge changes. Visual polish cannot reduce it. | Sweller 2019. (V1) | Same |
| Show interacting parts one at a time first, then together. | Pollock et al. 2002, cited in Sweller 2019. (V1) | Same |
| Context at the start reduces the load during reading. | N = 300. Without titles, readers needed 550 or 321 ms per idea. With titles, 209 or 152 ms. (V2) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2812925/ |

Rule: each section teaches one idea built from at most 3 or 4 new elements. Define each part before the text that shows the parts working together.

## 4. Worked examples

| Finding | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| For beginners, studying a worked example beats solving the same problem. | Barbieri et al. 2023: 55 studies, 181 effect sizes, g = 0.48. Mathematics and science. (V2) | https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1 |
| Correct examples alone gave larger effects than incorrect or mixed examples. | Same. | Same |
| Remove steps gradually, and ask "which principle is this step?". | Atkinson et al. 2003: medium to large effects on transfer, with no extra time. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.95.4.774 |
| Help that is good for beginners can hurt experts. | Tetzlaff et al. 2025: 60 studies. Beginners with high help d = 0.505. Experts d = -0.428. (V3) | Publisher page refused access |
| A short "predict first" task can help conceptual learning. | Sinha and Kapur 2021: 53 studies, g = 0.36. The result is contested. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105 |

Rule: write for the beginner. Give the expert a way to skip. Put a complete worked example directly after each concept.

## 5. Multimedia principles (Mayer), with independent checks

Effect sizes are Cohen's d or Hedges' g.

| Principle | Meaning for a web page | Mayer 2021 | Independent meta-analysis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coherence | Remove material that is not needed | 0.86 | -0.16 to -0.48 for adding irrelevant details (section 10) |
| Signaling | Show the structure: headings, a few bold terms | 0.70 | 0.38 (Alpizar 2020, V2) |
| Spatial contiguity | Put words next to the thing they describe | 0.82 | 0.63 (Schroeder and Cenkci 2018, V2) |
| Multimedia | Words plus relevant pictures | 1.35 | 0.39 (Guo 2020, V1). 0.23 (science texts 2025, V2) |
| Pre-training | Name and define the parts first | 0.78 | Not checked |
| Segmenting | Short sections | 0.67 | "Small to medium" (Rey 2019, V2). Expect little gain on text. |
| Personalization | Write "you" and "your" | 1.00 | 0.30 retention, 0.54 transfer (Ginns 2013, V2) |
| Modality, temporal contiguity | About speech and animation | 1.00, 1.31 | Not applicable to silent pages |
| Redundancy | Do not repeat a self-explaining diagram in text | 0.10 | Inconsistent |

Source for Mayer 2021: https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf

Boundary for signaling: it works "when visual and verbal signals are used sparingly". Much bold text removes the effect.

Null result: a 2025 study (N = 65) found no learning difference between rich, sparse, and no multimedia when the lesson was already clear and well structured. (V2)

## 6. Examples, comparison, analogy

| Finding | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Comparing two cases side by side beats studying them one at a time. | Alfieri et al. 2013: 57 experiments, d = 0.50, 95% interval 0.44 to 0.56. Larger when the principle is given after the comparison. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.775712 |
| Two cases that differ in one important feature prepare the reader for the explanation. | Schwartz and Bransford 1998: better predictions one week later. (V1) | http://aaalab.stanford.edu/assets/papers/earlier/A_time_for_telling.pdf |
| Concrete first, then abstract. | Goldstone and Son 2005 (V1). Fyfe et al. 2014 review (V2). Evidence for adults is medium to low. | https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327809jls1401_4 |
| Examples beat definitions alone. | Rawson et al. 2015: effect sizes 0.74 to 1.67. | https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1071985 |
| People rarely apply an analogy without help. | Gick and Holyoak 1980, 1983. (R) | Not verified |

Rule for analogies: state the mapping in plain words. Say which parts correspond. Say where the analogy stops being true.

## 7. Pictures and diagrams

| Finding | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant graphics help moderately. | g = 0.39 (39 studies, V1). g = 0.232 (63 studies, 7,621 students, V2). | https://doi.org/10.1177/2332858420901696 |
| The function of the picture decides its value. Pictures that show organization or explanation help most. Decorative pictures do not help. | 2025 meta-analysis (V2). Mayer and Gallini 1990: only "parts-and-steps" illustrations improved problem solving (V1). | https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.82.4.715 |
| Simplified diagrams support understanding best. | Butcher 2006. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.98.1.182 |
| A diagram works because it places related information in one location. | Larkin and Simon 1987. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1551-6708.1987.tb00863.x |
| "Dual coding" as the reason is contested. | Higdon et al. 2024. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1177/17470218241235520 |

Rule: every figure shows structure, sequence, or cause. No stock photos. Justify a picture by its function.

## 8. Retrieval practice and other study techniques

Dunlosky et al. 2013 utility ratings (V1, full text):

| Rating | Techniques |
|---|---|
| High | Practice testing. Distributed practice (spacing). |
| Moderate | Elaborative interrogation ("why is this true?"). Self-explanation. Interleaved practice. |
| Low | Summarization. Highlighting. Keyword mnemonic. Imagery for text. Rereading. |

Source: https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2013-dunlosky.pdf

| Finding | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice beats rereading. | Rowland 2014: g = 0.50, interval 0.42 to 0.58. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559 |
| Feedback matters. | With feedback g = 0.73. Without feedback g = 0.39. | Same |
| If the question is too difficult and there is no feedback, the benefit disappears. | Initial success 50% or lower, no feedback: g = 0.03. | Same |
| Recall questions beat recognition questions. | Cued recall 0.61. Recognition (multiple choice) 0.29. | Same |
| Questions direct attention to the content they ask about. | Hamaker 1986. Higher-order questions have a broader benefit. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543056002212 |
| Spacing is robust, but one page cannot create it. | Cepeda et al. 2006: 317 experiments. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354 |
| Interleaving helps the reader separate similar categories. It does not help with explanatory text. | Brunmair and Richter 2019: overall g = 0.42. Expository text: not significant. (V1) | See report |
| Self-explanation prompts help, but cost time. | Bisra et al. 2018: g = 0.55. (V1) | https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2018-bisra.pdf |

Rule: end each main section with 1 to 3 questions. Most readers must be able to answer them. The answer includes the reasoning. Ask "why" and "how" and "calculate", not "which word did the text use".

## 9. Overview before detail

| Finding | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Context must come before the content. | Bransford and Johnson 1972: the topic before the text greatly increased recall. The topic after the text did not. (V3) | https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(72)80006-9 |
| Advance organizers help, but modestly. | Luiten et al. 1980: 135 studies. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312017002211 |
| Headings, overviews, and summaries change how readers store the content. | Lorch and Lorch 1995. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.87.4.537 |
| Asking "what do you already know?" works only if the reader has correct knowledge. | Hattan et al. 2023: 54 articles. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543221148478 |
| Readers with low knowledge need fully connected text. | McNamara et al. 1996. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1401_1 |

Rule: for a new topic, give the reader a concrete case to build on. Do not leave gaps in the reasoning on purpose.

## 10. Irrelevant interesting details

| Finding | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Interesting but irrelevant material reduces learning. | Rey 2012: 39 studies, retention d = 0.30, transfer d = 0.48 (V3). 2025 meta-analysis: 50 studies, g = -0.16 (V2). | https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10099-z |
| More irrelevant detail gives worse recall. | Two studies, N = 191 and N = 101. (V2) | See report |
| A label "this is a side note" removes most of the harm. | N = 248. (V2) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10176302/ |

Rule: "detail" for this reader means more depth on the main idea: more steps, more examples, exact values. It does not mean side topics. If a side note must stay, label it as a side note.

## 11. Easy reading and the illusion of understanding

| Finding | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A feeling of ease during reading does not predict what is remembered. | Bjork and Bjork (V1). Kornell and Bjork 2008 (V1). Rozenblit and Keil 2002: illusion of explanatory depth, 12 studies (V1). | https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000 |
| The useful difficulties are retrieval, spacing, variation, and generation. Text that is hard to read is not useful. | Hard fonts: three replications failed. Meta-analysis: recall d = -0.01. (V3) | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7854329/ |
| Reading on a screen is slightly worse than on paper for informational text. | Delgado et al. 2018: 54 studies, 171,055 participants, g = -0.21. (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2018.09.003 |
| Links inside the text add decisions for the reader. | DeStefano and LeFevre 2007. (V3, low confidence) | — |

Balance rule: make perception and language as easy as possible. Put the effort into thinking tasks: recall, compare, predict, explain.

## 12. Correcting wrong ideas

| Finding | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| A text that states the wrong idea, says it is wrong, and explains the correct idea beats a plain explanation. | Schroeder and Kucera 2022: 44 comparisons, n = 3,869, g = 0.41. (V1) | https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-021-09656-z |
| Repeating a wrong idea in order to correct it is not a large risk. | Swire-Thompson et al. 2020: "backfire effects are not a robust empirical phenomenon". (V1) | https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jarmac.2020.06.006 |

Rule: the explanation of why the wrong idea fails must be present. A correction without the reason is not enough.

## 13. Ideas to avoid

| Idea | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Learning styles ("visual learner") | "Virtually no evidence" | Pashler et al. 2008 (V1) |
| Hard-to-read fonts improve memory | Replications failed | Section 11 |
| "People remember 10% of what they read and 90% of what they do" | Invented numbers | Subramony et al. 2014 (V2) |
| "7 plus or minus 2" as a limit for list items | Misreading of Miller | Section 3 |
| Correcting a wrong idea makes it stronger | Not robust | Section 12 |
| More multimedia is always better | Null result on clear content | Section 5 |
| Highlighting and rereading as study methods | Low utility | Dunlosky 2013 (V1) |

## 14. Ranked list of principles

The ranking considers strength of evidence, independent replication, and fit to a static web page.

| Rank | Principle | Best independent effect size |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remove everything that the explanation does not need | -0.16 to -0.48 for irrelevant details |
| 2 | Put words next to the thing they describe | 0.63 |
| 3 | Use only explanatory pictures: structure, steps, cause | 0.23 to 0.39 |
| 4 | Show the structure, with few signals | 0.33 to 0.53 |
| 5 | Worked example directly after each concept | 0.48 |
| 6 | Retrieval questions with feedback | 0.50 (0.73 with feedback) |
| 7 | One idea per section, 3 to 4 new elements, parts defined first | Theory, no direct number |
| 8 | Compare two cases side by side, then state the principle | 0.50 |
| 9 | Write for beginners, give experts a way to skip | 0.5 for beginners |
| 10 | Context before detail | Modest |
| 11 | Correct known wrong ideas explicitly | 0.41 |
| 12 | Add a self-check against the illusion of understanding | — |
| 13 | Concrete case first, then the general rule | Medium to low evidence for adults |
| 14 | Write "you" and "your" | 0.30 to 0.54 |
| 15 | Easy to perceive, effort in thinking | — |

## 15. Gaps: what was not verified

- Several publisher sites refused access (ScienceDirect, Wiley, SAGE, PubMed).
- Sundararajan and Adesope 2020: the exact overall effect size was not read directly.
- Tetzlaff et al. 2025: values come from two secondary sources.
- Adesope et al. 2017 testing meta-analysis: the abstract has no numbers.
- Recalled only: van Gog et al. 2011 (example-problem pairs), Gick and Holyoak (analogy), exact scores of Bransford and Johnson 1972.
- Gap in the research itself: almost all experiments use short lessons, students, and tests soon after learning. Evidence on adults reading web explanations at their own speed is rare.
