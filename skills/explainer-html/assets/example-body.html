How does compound interest make money grow faster over time?

Written {{today}} · Reading time: about {{minutes}} minutes · You need to know: percentages

The short answer

With compound interest, you earn interest on your earlier interest, not only on your starting money. The result is growth that becomes faster every year.

Example: US$10,000 at 5% per year becomes $43,219 after 30 years with compound interest. With simple interest it becomes $25,000. The difference is $18,219.

Assumptions on this page
  • The rate is 5% in every year.
  • The bank adds the interest one time per year.
  • You do not add money and you do not remove money.

1. One example: US$10,000 grows to US$11,576 in 3 years

You put US$10,000 in a bank account. The bank pays 5% interest (money the bank pays you for keeping your money there) every year. You do not take any money out. The balance (the amount of money in the account at one moment) is US$10,000 at the start.

Worked example
  1. Year 1: $10,000 × 0.05 = $500 interest. New balance: $10,500. 5% written as a decimal is 0.05.
  2. Year 2: $10,500 × 0.05 = $525 interest. New balance: $11,025. The bank now calculates 5% of $10,500, not of $10,000. The extra $25 is interest on last year's $500.
  3. Year 3: $11,025 × 0.05 = $551.25 interest. New balance: $11,576.25. The balance is larger again, so the interest is larger again.

Result: the interest grows each year: $500, then $525, then $551.25.

2. The rule: each year multiplies the balance by 1.05

Each year, the balance is multiplied by 1.05. After n years, the balance is multiplied by 1.05 exactly n times.

final balance = starting money × (1 + rate)^years
$11,576.25    = $10,000        × (1 + 0.05)^3

Compare the two types of interest

The example in section 1 uses compound interest: the bank calculates the interest on the current balance. The other type is simple interest: the bank calculates the interest on the starting money only.

Simple interest

Interest is calculated on the starting money only.

Every year: the same $500.

Compound interest

Interest is calculated on the current balance.

Every year: more than the year before.

What changes when the bank adds interest every month

Some banks add interest 12 times per year. Then each month multiplies the balance by (1 + 0.05 ÷ 12). After 3 years the balance is US$11,614.72. This is US$38.47 more than with yearly interest.

3. The difference grows every year: US$1,289 after 10 years, US$18,219 after 30

Guess first

After 30 years at 5%, how much larger is the compound result than the simple result? Choose a number before you continue: 10% larger, 40% larger, or 70% larger.

Answer to the guess: $43,219 ÷ $25,000 = 1.73. The compound result is about 73% larger.

4. Wrong idea corrected: 30 years at 5% give 332% growth, not 150%

Common wrong idea

Wrong: "5% per year for 30 years is 5 × 30 = 150% growth in total."

Correct: the growth is 332%. The balance goes from $10,000 to $43,219.

Why the wrong idea fails: 5 × 30 adds the rates. But each year's 5% applies to a larger balance, so the yearly factors must be multiplied: 1.05 × 1.05 × … (30 times) = 4.32.

5. Limits: real rates change, and inflation and tax reduce the result

Limits
  • The rate is not fixed in real life. Bank rates change. The numbers above assume 5% every year.
  • Inflation is not included. $43,219 in 30 years buys less than $43,219 today.
  • Tax is not included. In many countries you pay tax on interest.

6. Check your understanding

Try to answer before you open each answer.
  1. You have $2,000 at 10% compound interest per year. What is the balance after 2 years?
    Show the answer

    $2,420.

    Year 1: $2,000 × 1.10 = $2,200. Year 2: $2,200 × 1.10 = $2,420. If your answer was $2,400, you used simple interest. See section 1.

  2. Why is the compound line in the chart a curve, and the simple line straight?
    Show the answer

    Simple interest adds the same amount every year, so the line rises at a constant rate. Compound interest adds a larger amount every year, so the line becomes steeper.

7. Recap, glossary, and sources

Recap
  1. Compound interest is calculated on the current balance, which includes earlier interest.
  2. The formula is: final balance = starting money × (1 + rate)^years.
  3. The difference between compound and simple interest is small at first and large later: $1,289 after 10 years, $18,219 after 30 years.

Glossary

Balance
The amount of money in the account at one moment.
Compound interest
Interest that is calculated on the current balance. The current balance includes earlier interest.
Interest
Money the bank pays you for keeping your money there.
Simple interest
Interest that is calculated on the starting money only.
